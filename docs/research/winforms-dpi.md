# WinForms and per-monitor scaling

How WinForms (.NET 10, PerMonitorV2) behaves when a window opens on a monitor whose scaling differs from the main monitor's. Updated in place; wrong findings are marked wrong, not deleted.

## 2026-11-14: a new form on the 100 % monitor (measured, To do 3 step A)
Setup: the main monitor (laptop) is at 150 %, so the system DPI is 144; DISPLAY6 is at 100 %, DISPLAY5 at 150 %. The grid window: `FixedToolWindow`, `AutoScaleMode.None`, sizes in physical pixels from `GetDpiForMonitor`, fonts in `GraphicsUnit.Pixel`.

- Logged with `OnDpiChanged` and `DeviceDpi`: a form created on DISPLAY6 gets "DpiChanged 96 → 144" while its window is created, whether its `Location` was the monitor's corner or its middle, and reports `DeviceDpi` 144 in `OnLoad`. The first move in `OnLoad` then gives "DpiChanged 144 → 96".
- That second change: WinForms resized the Labels' fonts from 12 px to 8 px (×96/144, although they were set in pixels), and Windows kept the window's outer size, so the client area grew from 256x184 to 262x201. The tiles' positions and sizes (`AutoScaleMode.None`) did not change.
- On DISPLAY5 (150 %, like the main monitor): no DPI change at all.
- Step A's first version (borderless, never moved after its window existed) showed right-sized titles on DISPLAY6: no move, so no second change (inferred from the log above; that version wasn't logged).
- Laying the grid out again in `OnDpiChanged` after a move between monitors (directly, or later with `BeginInvoke`) also gave titles at 2/3 size; with `BeginInvoke` the window also kept its old size (not explained).
- What works (measured at 100 % and 150 %): text drawn by the form itself (`TextRenderer.DrawText` in `OnPaint`, with its own pixel-size fonts), and in `OnLoad`: move, set `ClientSize`, place again. The grid is kept on its monitor while dragged (`WM_MOVING`), so it never changes DPI after that. In src/TrayAnywhere/GridForm.cs.

## 2026-11-14: the grid not always on top (measured, To do 3 step B)
Not about scaling, but WinForms window behaviour of the same grid.

- Seen by the owner: with the grid open on the right monitor, VS Code came to the front (after Exit in Steam's menu) and covered the grid. A window logger showed the grid neither closed nor moved: still visible at its place, but without `WS_EX_TOPMOST`, below VS Code in the z-order. Then the hotkey closed the hidden grid, and a second press opened a new one, also without the flag.
- Probe (a WinForms program in a temp folder that loads the app's DLL and uses its classes through reflection; the flag read with `GetWindowLong` every 10 ms): `GridForm` shown directly (with and without icons, on both 150 % monitors, and when it took the front with `SetForegroundWindow`) and `App.OpenOrCloseGrid` called directly: on top in 11 of 12 runs (the odd one came right after a hotkey run; not explained). Opened through a registered hotkey (the app's `Hotkey` class with Ctrl+Alt+F12, pressed with `keybd_event`): not on top, 6 of 6. The real app with Alt+`: not on top from the moment its window existed (87 ms after the press).
- Trace of the window messages (a copy of `GridForm` that logs them), opened through the hotkey: the window is created without the flag (already off in `OnHandleCreated`, though `TopMost` is true); it is activated while being created (`WM_ACTIVATE` before `OnLoad`); while it shows, Windows places it three times behind another window (`WM_WINDOWPOSCHANGING` with that window as `insertAfter`); `OnShown` comes after all of that. Why the flag is left out on this path is not known. Probably it has to do with the app being allowed to take the front after a hotkey (inferred).
- Fix (in src/TrayAnywhere/GridForm.cs `KeepOnTop`): `SetWindowPos(HWND_TOPMOST)` with `SWP_NOACTIVATE`, only when the flag is missing, in `OnShown` and at every refresh. With it: on top in 4 of 4 probe runs (3 through the hotkey, 1 direct). Only when missing, because setting it also lifts the grid above other always-on-top windows, such as the owner's notification popups in the same corner. Not through the `TopMost` property: its `SetWindowPos` has no `SWP_NOACTIVATE`, so it would also activate the grid. (`TopMost = true` on an open grid did bring the flag back, measured once.)
- The owner's notification popup script ran into something similar earlier: a popup sank below normal windows while keeping its flag; it now sets `HWND_TOPMOST` again when it finds itself sunk.
- Not tested, though first reported as tested: a grid without `ShowInTaskbar = false` (to drop its hidden owner window), and setting `TopMost` in `OnActivated`. Both test runs loaded the app's original DLL instead of the changed copy: the probe's folder held a copy of TrayAnywhere.dll from a project reference, and `Assembly.LoadFrom` with another path returned that one. For later probes: no reference to the DLL under test, and print `Assembly.Location`.

## 2026-11-14: the floating buttons (measured, To do 3 step C)
- The button is a borderless form (`FormBorderStyle.None`, `AutoScaleMode.None`), with the same "move, set the size, place again" in `OnLoad` as the grid. Measured with a window probe right after start: 32x32 at 96 DPI on DISPLAY6 (100 %), 48x48 at 144 DPI on DISPLAY5 (150 %), each 12 px (at 100 %) from the free area's right and bottom edges, as planned.
- Always on top without taking the focus: `ShowWithoutActivation` plus `WS_EX_TOPMOST` set in `CreateParams`, not `TopMost`. Measured once: both buttons had the flag right after start, and the window in front (VS Code) stayed in front. Why not `TopMost`: WinForms activates a `TopMost` form when it shows, whatever `ShowWithoutActivation` says (known WinForms behaviour, not tested here). `OnShown` also calls `TopWindow.KeepOnTop`, in case Windows drops the flag as it did for the grid.
- The grid opened by a click on a button (posted `WM_LBUTTONDOWN`/`UP`, so not the real mouse's foreground path): always on top, right edge at the button's right edge, bottom 8 px (at 100 %) above it, on both monitors; a second click closed it; a click on the other monitor's button moved it there (measured once). The real click is the owner's test.
- After the change to "the grid always opens at its button" (same probe, measured once each): with a dragged grid place saved for DISPLAY5, the grid still opened at the button. With the DISPLAY6 button moved into the top half of its monitor (a temporary settings.json; the owner's file restored after, same hash), the arrow drew as a v and the grid opened 8 px below the button, right edges lined up. Not probed: the grid following a dragged button (a drag needs the real mouse).
- The button following a dragged grid, probed with a faked title-bar drag: `SendMessage(WM_ENTERSIZEMOVE)`, `SetWindowPos` moves from another process, `SendMessage(WM_EXITSIZEMOVE)`. WinForms then raises `OnResizeBegin`, `OnLocationChanged` per move and `OnResizeEnd`, as for a real drag (no `WM_MOVING`, so the grid's own keep-on-its-monitor isn't exercised that way). Measured once on DISPLAY5: in the bottom half the button sat 12 px below the grid, right edges lined up; in the top half 12 px above it; at the top edge the button stayed at the edge, and on release the grid moved to 12 px below it. The new button place was saved (the owner's settings.json backed up and restored, same hash).
- The grid's visible frame sits inside its window rectangle: ~~in a screenshot at 150 % its right edge is about 7 px left of the button's, though both rectangles end at the same x (estimated from the screenshot).~~ Measured in the GUI test's screenshots (To do 4 step B): 9 px left of the button's at 150 %, 7 px at 100 %. It's probably Windows' invisible border around framed windows. `DwmGetWindowAttribute(DWMWA_EXTENDED_FRAME_BOUNDS)` would give the visible frame, if the owner wants the edges lined up exactly.

## 2026-11-20: the settings window, with standard controls (measured, To do 17 step A)
- A `FixedDialog` form with a `TableLayoutPanel` and a `CheckBox`, `AutoScaleMode.None`, the font set in pixels in `OnLoad` after the first move (as for the grid): right-sized text on both monitors, 150 % (DISPLAY5) and 100 % (DISPLAY6), in the screenshots.
- The form's own `AutoSize` sized the window only after `OnLoad` had placed it, 9 px off the middle at 150 % (1 run). Now `ClientSize` comes from the table's `PreferredSize` in `OnLoad`.
- Moved to the other monitor with `SetWindowPos` from the test (the fake drag of the GUI test, no `WM_MOVING`): WinForms rescaled the text, but the window kept its size, 255x127 from 150 % to 100 % (empty space around the text) and 198x88 from 100 % to 150 % (text cut off). One run each way. Not tried with a real drag. So the settings window stays on its monitor while dragged, as the grid does (`TopWindow.KeepOnMonitor`), and "Settings..." on another monitor opens it there.
- At 150 % its title, "tray-anywhere settings", was cut off while the window was only as wide as its checkbox: a minimum width of 220 px at 100 % (seen in the screenshots).

## 2026-11-21: the button's look (measured, To do 17 step D)
- WinForms reads a form's `CreateParams` already in `Control`'s constructor, before the derived class's constructor body has run: `FloatingButton.CreateParams` reading the `look` field crashed with a NullReferenceException there (stack: `Control..ctor` → `get_CreateParams`). So always on top is copied into a `bool` field of its own, false at that point; no window exists yet, so it does no harm.
- A flat `RadioButton` with `Appearance = Button` as a color square: `FlatAppearance.CheckedBackColor` set to its own color, so the picked one keeps it (WinForms' default for a picked flat button not tried), `BorderColor` changed at each `CheckedChanged` for the frame (light when picked, grey otherwise, so the dark square shows on the dark window). Seen in the screenshots at 150 % and 100 %. The greyed (disabled) look while the button is off wasn't in the screenshots: the owner's hand test.
